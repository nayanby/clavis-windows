Phenotypes

  • HCM: unexplained ventricular hypertrophy. DCM: LV dilation with systolic dysfunction.
  • Non-dilated LV cardiomyopathy: nonischemic scar/fatty replacement without dilation, or unexplained global systolic dysfunction without scar.
  • Restrictive: stiff ventricles, impaired filling, enlarged atria; EF may remain preserved. Exclude coronary disease and loading conditions sufficient to explain the abnormality.

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

  • Diagnosis: unexplained adult wall thickness ≥15 mm; 13–14 mm may be significant with a familial/genetic context. Children require size-adjusted criteria.
  • LVOT gradient: ≥30 mmHg defines obstruction; ≥50 mmHg is an important threshold for advanced treatment in persistently symptomatic patients.
  • Dynamic obstruction: reduced preload/afterload or increased contractility worsens the gradient. Standing/Valsalva usually increases the murmur. Exercise echo detects latent obstruction.
  • Systolic anterior mitral motion: contributes to obstruction and mitral regurgitation.
  • Symptoms: nonvasodilating beta blocker first; verapamil/diltiazem if unsuitable or ineffective. Avoid these alternatives with hypotension, severe resting symptoms or very high gradients.
  • Persistent symptoms: myosin inhibitor, disopyramide with AV nodal blockade, or septal reduction. Intrinsic mitral/papillary abnormalities favor surgical myectomy.
  • Mavacamten: monitor EF and interactions; initiation with EF <55% is not recommended; interrupt if EF <50% or heart failure develops.
  • Sudden-death risk: previous arrest/sustained ventricular arrhythmia supports secondary-prevention ICD assessment. Primary-prevention markers include arrhythmic syncope, family HCM-related sudden death, wall thickness ≥30 mm, apical aneurysm and EF <50%; NSVT/extensive scar add risk information.
  • Clinical AF: anticoagulate without relying on a low conventional stroke-risk score. Gradient reduction does not remove arrhythmic risk.

Dilated cardiomyopathy

  • Reversible contributors: alcohol, sustained tachyarrhythmia, cardiotoxic drugs and inflammation. Genetic susceptibility may coexist; alcohol-related disease requires abstinence.
  • HFrEF treatment: ARNI/ACE inhibitor/ARB, evidence-based beta blocker, mineralocorticoid antagonist and SGLT2 inhibitor as tolerated; diuretics for congestion. EF recovery alone does not justify withdrawal.
  • LMNA: early conduction disease, atrial arrhythmia and VT; EF >35% does not exclude substantial arrhythmic risk. Assess genotype, NSVT, block and scar when considering an ICD.
  • Peripartum cardiomyopathy: otherwise unexplained systolic failure near late pregnancy/postpartum, usually EF <45%; dilation is not required. Avoid ACE inhibitors/ARBs/ARNIs during pregnancy; postpartum treatment depends on lactation. Selected bromocriptine protocols require attention to anticoagulation.

Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy

  • RV disease: regional akinesia/dyskinesia, structural dysfunction, anterior T-wave inversion and ventricular arrhythmias; diagnose using combined imaging, electrical, family and genetic evidence.
  • PKP2: commonly RV-predominant. DSP: often LV-predominant, with subepicardial/ring-like scar and myocarditis-like episodes.
  • High-intensity endurance exercise can accelerate disease and arrhythmia, including before overt dysfunction. A normal echo does not eliminate inherited risk.

Normal pericardial thickness does not exclude constriction. Dip-and-plateau or diastolic pressure equalization alone is not diagnostic.

Cardiac amyloidosis

  • Clues: increased wall thickness, conduction disease, carpal tunnel syndrome, neuropathy, low voltage or apical-sparing strain; none is independently diagnostic.
  • Exclude AL: serum free light chains + serum immunofixation + urine immunofixation. Electrophoresis alone is insufficient; CKD alters light-chain interpretation.
  • Nonbiopsy ATTR: compatible cardiac phenotype + grade 2–3 bone-tracer myocardial uptake, confirmed appropriately with SPECT, + exclusion of monoclonal light-chain disease.
  • Monoclonal abnormality: a positive PYP scan cannot establish ATTR alone; obtain tissue confirmation/typing as needed.
  • AL: urgent plasma-cell-directed treatment. ATTR: tafamidis/acoramidis stabilize TTR; vutrisiran suppresses production where indicated. Genetic testing separates hereditary from wild-type ATTR.

Storage disease and iron

  • Fabry: GLA/alpha-galactosidase A deficiency; LVH with neuropathic pain, angiokeratomas, reduced sweating, renal disease or stroke. Enzyme activity may be normal in affected women; confirm genetically. Enzyme replacement or an amenable-variant chaperone treats the cause.
  • PRKAG2: altered AMPK signaling with glycogen accumulation; LVH + pre-excitation + progressive conduction disease.
  • Danon: X-linked LAMP2 disease; severe cardiomyopathy, skeletal myopathy, elevated CK and pre-excitation; early advanced-HF assessment.
  • Cardiac iron at 1.5 T: T2* <20 ms indicates overload; <10 ms is severe. Lower T2* means more iron; ferritin alone cannot quantify cardiac deposition. Treat with appropriate chelation.

Myocarditis

  • MRI: edema with nonischemic injury, often subepicardial inferolateral enhancement. Troponin elevation or normal coronaries alone does not establish myocarditis.
  • Biopsy: especially important with shock, rapid deterioration, serious VT or advanced block when inflammatory subtype changes treatment.
  • Giant-cell myocarditis: rapidly progressive failure, ventricular arrhythmia and block; biopsy diagnosis, combined immunosuppression and early mechanical-support/transplant assessment. Steroids alone are generally insufficient.

Cardiac sarcoidosis

  • Presentation: unexplained high-grade AV block, VT or ventricular dysfunction; extracardiac disease may be absent.
  • CMR LGE: myocardial injury/scar. Prepared FDG-PET: active inflammation. Patchy involvement limits unguided biopsy sensitivity.
  • Treatment: immunosuppression for appropriate active disease; assess pacing/ICD needs separately. Controlling inflammation does not erase arrhythmogenic scar.

Takotsubo syndrome

  • Transient dysfunction: apical or other ballooning, often beyond one coronary territory; stress trigger is common, not obligatory. Exclude ACS/myocarditis. Recovery does not prevent acute shock, thrombus or arrhythmia.
  • Shock with LVOT obstruction: avoid positive inotropes; carefully selected fluid may help if underfilled without pulmonary congestion. Pump failure, RV involvement and severe MR require different support; do not give beta blockers indiscriminately in shock.

Family screening and exercise

  • Pathogenic/likely pathogenic familial variant: counseling, targeted cascade testing and appropriate clinical screening of first-degree relatives.
  • VUS: not sufficient for predictive family testing or irreversible treatment; expert segregation studies may help. Continue phenotype/family-based surveillance.
  • Sport: individualized HCM advice; high-intensity endurance sport is particularly concerning in arrhythmogenic disease. Persistent ventricular arrhythmia after myocarditis prevents intensive-sport clearance.
  • Trabeculation alone: does not establish cardiomyopathy in an athlete with reassuring function, rhythm and family assessment.
